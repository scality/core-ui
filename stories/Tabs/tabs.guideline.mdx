# Tabs

Tabs split one subject into views the user switches between, keeping the page and
its context. Each `Tab` owns a route, so a view is linkable and survives a reload.

## How much inset the content gets

A tab insets its content on all four sides. The right side is the one the tab
cannot decide alone: whether a gutter is already there is known to the content
and to nothing else. `padding` is how the call site says so.

| Value               | Pick it when                                                                                                         |
| ------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| `'all'` _(default)_ | The content owns none of its own edges and leaves all four to the tab.                                               |
| `'exceptRight'`     | The content owns its right edge because it scrolls itself and reserves a gutter for its scrollbar.                   |
| `'none'`            | The content draws to the tab's frame on all four sides — a table or a list, or anything painting its own background. |

**The case to recognise for `'exceptRight'`** is a `Form` with
`layout={{ kind: 'tab' }}`: a fixed header with the fields scrolling under it.
Such a form reserves room for its scrollbar at its own right edge, so left on
`'all'` the tab's inset stacks on top of that reserve and the scrollbar ends up
in the middle of a gap instead of near the edge.

```tsx
<Tab path="/settings" label="Settings" padding="exceptRight">
  <SettingsForm />
</Tab>
```

The rule follows the content, not the component that happens to be the tab's
direct child. A form behind a wrapper still owns the right edge, and the wrapper
cannot pass that fact up — so the call site sets `padding` itself.

Content with no gutter of its own and nothing to paint to the frame keeps the
default; there is no reason to set `padding` on most tabs.

`withoutPadding` is the former spelling of `padding="none"`. It still works and
is ignored when `padding` is also set, but new code uses `padding`.
